ENERGY FOR A CHANGING WORLD
01 — THE BIG PICTURE

A powerful idea,
made practical.

Every hour, the Sun sends an enormous amount of energy toward Earth. Solar technology captures a small part of that incoming energy and turns it into electricity or heat that people can use.

Photovoltaic solar panels generate electricity directly from light. They can be installed on homes, schools, warehouses, parking structures, and large open areas. A single installation may help power one building; thousands of installations can contribute to an electricity network.

Solar power is not a complete answer to every energy challenge. It varies with daylight and weather, requires materials and manufacturing, and often works best alongside other energy sources and storage. Understanding both its strengths and its limitations is what makes the technology so interesting.

02 — THE PHOTOVOLTAIC EFFECT

From a ray of light
to a room full of power.

A solar cell does not store sunlight. It uses the energy in light to set electrical charges in motion. That process is known as the photovoltaic effect.

01

Light reaches the cell

A solar panel contains many photovoltaic cells, most commonly made using silicon. When sunlight reaches a cell, particles of light called photons transfer energy to the material. Some of that energy is absorbed and can free electrons to move.

光
02

Electrical charges are separated

The cell is designed with an internal electric field, created by carefully treating different layers of semiconductor material. This field helps direct electrons and positively charged spaces in opposite directions, producing a voltage.

±
03

Current flows through a circuit

When the cell is connected to an electrical circuit, electrons can travel through the external path. Their movement is an electric current. Multiple cells are connected together to form a panel, and panels are connected to create an array.

↗
04

An inverter makes the electricity useful

Solar panels produce direct current, or DC. Most buildings and the electricity grid use alternating current, or AC. An inverter converts the electricity into the form required by household equipment or the grid.

∿
One important distinction

Solar photovoltaic panels generate electricity. Solar thermal systems, by contrast, collect the Sun's heat, often to warm water or another fluid. Both use solar energy, but they do different jobs.

03 — THE SYSTEM

More than
a panel.

A working solar installation is a collection of components. Each has a specific role, from capturing sunlight to safely delivering electricity where it is needed.

A / GENERATION

Photovoltaic modules

Cells are sealed into modules to protect them from the weather. Modules are wired together into strings and arrays to reach the voltage and power level required by the system.

DC→AC
B / CONVERSION

The inverter

The inverter converts DC into AC and monitors the system. Some designs also manage battery charging, grid interaction, and system safety.

C / STORAGE

Batteries

A battery can store surplus electricity for later use. It may supply power after sunset or provide backup during an outage when the system is designed for that purpose.

HOMEGRID
D / CONNECTION

Metering and the grid

Grid-connected systems can use electricity from the network when solar production is low and may export surplus power when local rules and the connection agreement allow it.

04 — PANEL TECHNOLOGY

Different materials.
Different possibilities.

Not all solar panels are built in the same way. The best option depends on the available space, budget, climate, installation design, and the performance required.

TECHNOLOGYWHAT IT ISWHERE IT FITS

Monocrystalline silicon

Made from silicon with a single-crystal structure. These modules are widely used and often offer high conversion efficiency.

Common on homes, businesses, and utility-scale solar farms where space and output matter.

Polycrystalline silicon

Made from silicon containing multiple crystal structures. This technology was historically a common lower-cost option.

Found in older installations and some markets, although monocrystalline products have become widespread.

Thin-film

Uses very thin layers of photovoltaic material deposited onto a supporting surface. There are several thin-film chemistries.

Can suit certain large-area, lightweight, or specialized applications, depending on the product.

Bifacial modules

Designed to collect light from both the front and rear surfaces. Reflected light from the ground can add to production.

Often used in open installations with suitable mounting height and reflective surroundings.

05 — WHEN THE SUN CHANGES

Sunlight is not
constant.

Solar output changes throughout the day and across the seasons. System designers estimate production using location, orientation, tilt, shading, weather patterns, and equipment specifications.

What affects production?

Time of day: Output usually rises after sunrise, reaches a peak around the middle part of the day, and falls toward sunset. The exact peak depends on location, season, weather, and panel orientation.

Clouds and temperature: Panels can still generate electricity under cloud, but usually produce less. Very high cell temperatures can also reduce the output of many conventional modules.

Shade and dirt: Trees, chimneys, nearby buildings, dust, and debris can reduce generation. System layout and appropriate maintenance help manage these effects.

Direction and tilt: The best orientation depends on the site's latitude and the pattern of electricity use. A professional assessment should account for local conditions rather than rely on a universal angle.

06 — STORAGE AND RELIABILITY

Save today's
sun for later.

Solar panels generally produce the most electricity when the Sun is shining. But many homes and businesses use electricity early in the morning, in the evening, and overnight.

Battery energy storage can help bridge the gap. When solar production is greater than immediate demand, a compatible system can direct some of the surplus electricity into a battery. Later, the stored energy can be discharged to supply selected loads or support the wider electricity system.

Storage is not automatically included with every solar installation. Batteries add cost, have a limited operating life, and need appropriate controls and safety equipment. The right size depends on the building's energy use, backup requirements, local electricity prices, and the system's design.

A reliable electricity system combines generation, networks, flexible demand, and storage.
07 — THE ENVIRONMENTAL PICTURE

Cleaner electricity.
Not impact-free.

01

During operation

Solar photovoltaic systems generate electricity without burning fuel at the point of generation. This means they do not release direct combustion emissions while producing power. Replacing electricity from fossil-fuel plants can reduce greenhouse gas emissions and air pollution.

02

Before installation

Solar panels require mining, processing, manufacturing, transport, and construction. These stages consume energy and materials and create environmental impacts. A fair comparison considers the entire life cycle, not just what happens on a sunny afternoon.

03

At end of life

Panels eventually degrade or become uneconomic to operate. Recycling can recover materials such as glass and aluminum, while more specialized processes can recover other valuable components. Collection systems and recycling capacity are important parts of responsible solar deployment.

Think in life cycles

Solar power is generally a low-carbon electricity source over its full life cycle compared with fossil-fuel generation. Its exact environmental footprint depends on manufacturing methods, materials, transport, location, performance, and end-of-life treatment.

08 — A BALANCED VIEW

The benefits,
and the trade-offs.

THE OPPORTUNITY

Why solar is valuable

  • Sunlight is a renewable energy resource.
  • Solar PV produces electricity without direct combustion emissions during operation.
  • Systems can be installed close to where electricity is used.
  • Operating costs can be relatively predictable after installation.
  • Solar can be combined with batteries, wind, hydro, and other sources.

THE CHALLENGE

What needs planning

  • Output varies with daylight, seasons, and weather.
  • Upfront equipment and installation costs can be substantial.
  • Manufacturing requires materials, energy, and land or factory space.
  • Grid upgrades and storage may be needed as solar capacity grows.
  • End-of-life collection and recycling must be managed responsibly.
09 — FROM IDEA TO INSTALLATION

What does a solar
project involve?

01

Understand the energy demand

Review electricity bills, daily usage patterns, and future needs such as an electric vehicle, heat pump, or business expansion. The aim is to understand when and how much electricity is used.

02

Assess the site

Check roof condition, available area, orientation, tilt, shading, structural capacity, and electrical connection. Local weather and solar resource data help estimate expected generation.

03

Design the system

Choose suitable modules, an inverter, mounting equipment, protection devices, and possibly battery storage. The design must follow local electrical and building requirements.

04

Install and connect

Qualified installers mount and wire the equipment, complete electrical checks, and arrange approvals or grid connection where required. Safety procedures are essential throughout the work.

05

Monitor and maintain

System monitoring helps identify unexpected drops in output. Periodic inspection, safe cleaning when needed, and prompt attention to faults can help the system perform as intended.

10 — SOLAR AROUND THE WORLD

One Sun.
Many local stories.

Solar power can be used in very different places: from rooftops in dense cities to utility-scale projects in sunny regions, and from remote communities to industrial sites.

How much electricity a system can produce depends on more than a country's reputation for sunshine. Latitude, cloud patterns, temperature, dust, seasonal changes, grid access, land availability, financing, and public policy all influence the economics and practicality of solar energy.

In places where the grid is weak or remote, smaller solar systems with batteries can provide electricity to homes, schools, clinics, and communication equipment. In cities, rooftop solar can make use of existing building surfaces rather than requiring a separate power-generation site.

11 — WHAT COMES NEXT

The next chapter
is still being written.

01 / MATERIALS

More advanced cells

Researchers are developing new cell designs and materials that may improve efficiency, lower manufacturing costs, or enable new applications. Perovskite-based cells and tandem designs are among the areas receiving significant research attention.

02 / DESIGN

Solar in more places

Building-integrated photovoltaics, floating solar, and agrivoltaics explore ways to combine electricity generation with buildings, water surfaces, or agriculture. Each approach has its own engineering and environmental considerations.

03 / ELECTRICITY

Smarter energy systems

Better forecasting, flexible electricity demand, batteries, and modern grid controls can help electricity networks manage changing renewable generation while maintaining reliability.

14 — COMMON QUESTIONS

Good questions
deserve clear answers.

A few common questions about what solar panels can and cannot do.

Do solar panels work on cloudy days?

Yes. Photovoltaic panels can generate electricity from diffuse daylight, not only direct sunshine. Their output is usually lower under heavy cloud, and the amount depends on cloud thickness, panel technology, and local conditions.

Can solar panels generate electricity at night?

Conventional photovoltaic panels need light to generate electricity, so they do not produce meaningful solar power at night. A battery can store electricity generated earlier, while a grid-connected building can draw electricity from the network.

How long do solar panels last?

Many manufacturers offer performance warranties of around 25 years, and panels can continue operating beyond that period. Their output generally declines gradually over time. Actual service life depends on product quality, climate, installation, and maintenance.

Do I need a battery with solar panels?

No. Many solar installations operate without a battery and remain connected to the electricity grid. A battery may be useful when backup power, greater self-consumption, or time-shifting electricity use is important.

Will a solar system work during a power cut?

Most standard grid-connected systems shut down during a grid outage to protect utility workers and equipment. Backup power requires a compatible inverter, suitable battery or other source, and a correctly configured isolation system.

Are solar panels completely environmentally harmless?

No energy technology has zero environmental impact. Solar panels require materials, manufacturing, transport, land or roof space, and end-of-life management. However, solar PV generally has much lower lifecycle greenhouse gas emissions than fossil-fuel electricity.

Can solar panels power an entire home?

A suitably designed system can produce as much electricity as a home uses over a chosen period, but production and consumption do not always happen at the same time. The result depends on the home's demand, location, roof, system size, grid rules, and whether storage is included.
